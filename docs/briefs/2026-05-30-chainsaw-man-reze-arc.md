## Suggested angles

### 1. "What the Reze Arc Gets Right That Season 1 Couldn't"
Reze Arc is the argument for the theatrical anime film format. Season 1 was good but scattered — adapting four arcs across 12 episodes meant emotional beats had to share time with plot setup. The film compresses Denji and Reze's entire arc into ~2 hours with nothing extraneous. Lean into how MAPPA used the theatrical canvas: the extended quiet scenes, the café sequences, the color grading that makes Reze's introductory scenes feel warm before the violence strips that warmth away. This is a piece about what the format unlocks emotionally, not just visually. The Letterboxd numbers back it up — audiences who came in cold on the film loved it. Tie into the streaming debut as the reason more people are experiencing this right now.

### 2. "Reze Arc Deserved More From Awards Season — Here's Why That Matters"
The May 23 Anime Awards handing Film of the Year to Infinity Castle is the freshest hook. The piece can run as a response to that outcome: not a hit piece on Demon Slayer (both films are excellent), but an argument that Reze Arc's cultural footprint — Letterboxd #1 of 2025, $191M box office, a film that made non-anime people care — represents exactly what anime achieving mainstream recognition looks like, and the awards circuit consistently built for franchise spectacle over focused storytelling. The Oscar/Golden Globe snubs layer in naturally. This has real teeth for a Saturday column without needing to be mean about it.

### 3. "Reading Reze Arc as Setup: What Happens Next for Chainsaw Man"
Now that the film is on Crunchyroll and the Assassins Arc anime is officially announced (but dateless), there's an appetite for the connective tissue piece. Walk through what Reze Arc sets up thematically — Denji's relationship with love and grief, the fracture in the Public Safety organization, Makima's long game — and what the Assassins Arc will likely do with that material. This angle is more manga-reader-friendly and works well for a column that trusts its audience. Include a light spoiler warning but don't sanitize the take. The stage play (summer 2026 in Japan) is a nice piece of color that shows the IP's cultural reach right now.
