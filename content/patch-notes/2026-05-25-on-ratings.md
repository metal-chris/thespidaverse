---
title: On ratings, and why ours belong to you now
date: 2026-05-25
category: Philosophy
---
I'm pulling my personal Web Rating off the public side of the site. It still exists internally and I'll keep one for every review, but you won't see a number on the page anymore.

A couple things pushed me here. I noticed I was reverse-engineering my own scores after the fact, assigning whatever number "felt right" once the review was done, or worse, writing toward a number I'd decided on first. On the reader side, putting a big 87 at the top mostly just gives people permission to skip what I actually wrote about it.

At the same time, the **Web Rating** isn't going away. It's just yours now. The slider at the bottom of every review collects scores from anyone reading, and the average is the only number that shows on the page. It only appears once five people have rated, which keeps a single early vote from defining a piece. Below that threshold the page tells you how many votes are still needed.

I'm not putting the average on cards or in social previews. That would just rebuild the glance-and-go problem from a different angle. The number lives on the article page itself, where it sits next to the actual writing it's responding to.

My personal scores are just a thing I keep for myself now. The Web Rating is the one that matters.
